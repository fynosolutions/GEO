Bubbling Good Times: Best Hotpot Restaurants in Manhattan

November 25th, 2025 · corner editorial

Manhattan's hotpot scene offers some of the most interactive and communal dining experiences in the city, from spicy Szechuan broths to delicate Japanese nabe. We've explored the bubbling cauldrons across neighborhoods to find spots that deliver not just great flavor, but atmosphere, creativity, and that special something that makes hotpot dining unforgettable. Whether you're craving Chinese dry pot with customizable spice levels, Japanese sukiyaki in a cozy setting, or all-you-can-eat feasts with friends, these restaurants transform a simple meal into a memorable experience.

MáLà Project

mala dry pot

122 1st Ave, New York • East Village

This dark, cave-like East Village spot specializes in spicy Chinese dry pot where you choose your own spice level and ingredients for a fully customizable experience. We love building our bowls with eggplant, dumplings, rice cakes, and a mix of proteins like round eye and pork belly - everything gets tossed in that signature numbing málà sauce. The tofu options are exceptional, and don't sleep on the spicy cocktails that complement the heat perfectly.

The intimate atmosphere makes it ideal for small groups, though here's an insider tip: the takeout experience might actually be better than dining in. Multiple locations across the city mean you're never too far from your dry pot fix, and the lightning-fast service keeps things moving even when it's packed.

Kimura

japanese nabe

31 St Marks Pl, New York • Ukrainian Village

When temperatures drop, we head straight to this cozy Japanese nabe spot in the East Village for soul-warming hot pot and killer cocktails. The sukiyaki broth here is legendary - rich, slightly sweet, and deeply savory - while the mentai mushroom trio udon brings creamy, umami-packed comfort in every bite. The teppan gyoza make perfect shareable starters, and there's yuzu everywhere on the menu if you're into that bright citrus kick.

Snag a seat at the window bar for prime people-watching while you slurp. The broth is so flavorful and filling that we always leave feeling completely satisfied (okay, maybe a little too full). It's become one of our go-to spots for chilly winter days when nothing else will do.

The Dolar Shop Hot Pot Manhattan

macau hotpot

55 3rd Ave, New York • East Village

Each diner gets their own individual pot here - choose your broth, then dive into a premium spread of wagyu, lamb, and fresh vegetables. The real stars are the unlimited sauce bar where you can craft your perfect dipping combination, and the dessert station complete with soft serve and traditional 冰粉 (iced jelly dessert) that transports you straight back to childhood. The meat quality impresses, and the flavorful soup bases provide a solid foundation for your DIY feast.

Fair warning: the Manhattan location isn't all-you-can-eat like the Flushing original, which makes it significantly pricier. If you want the full AYCE experience with all the bells and whistles, make the trek to Queens - it's worth it for the complete unlimited hotpot extravaganza.

99 Favor Taste

AYCE hotpot & KBBQ

285 Grand St, New York • Bowery

This sprawling Chinatown destination offers all-you-can-eat hotpot and Korean BBQ in one lively, contemporary space. We're talking an impressive selection of meats, an extensive sauce bar, and all the usual hot pot components you could want - plus here's a birthday hack: eat free when you come with paying guests. The huge variety of sides and the dual format (hotpot OR KBBQ, your choice) make it perfect for groups who can't decide what they're craving.

The moderate pricing makes this an accessible option for indulgent group dinners without completely breaking the bank. While it might not be at the very top of our hotpot hierarchy, it's definitely our go-to when we need something affordable that still delivers on atmosphere and selection for birthday celebrations or big gatherings.

Hakata TonTon

pork feet izakaya

43 Mott St, New York • Chinatown

This authentic Japanese spot in Chinatown serves beautiful, artfully presented nabe that's as pleasing to the eye as it is to the palate. The flavors are absolutely on point - we particularly love the yum cha set, sui gyoza, and kani harumaki as starters. Just know that portions run small and prices are high, so this works better as an appetizer situation or small bites before heading elsewhere for your main event.

Don't skip dessert though - the black sesame ice cream and green tea almond tofu are both excellent ways to end the meal. We appreciate the attention to presentation and authentic Japanese flavors, even if the value proposition isn't quite as strong as some other spots on this list.

Hometown Hotpot & BBQ

AYCE hotpot & BBQ

194 Grand St, New York • Manhattan

This spacious Little Italy spot (technically still in Chinatown's sphere) gives you the best of both worlds: DIY hot pot bubbling away while you grill meats on tabletop BBQs. Cook your own proteins and veggies exactly how you like them, wash it down with watermelon juice or cocktails, and enjoy the rowdy, energetic atmosphere that comes with interactive dining. The space can accommodate big groups easily, making it a popular choice for celebrations.

It's become a go-to for large gatherings in the neighborhood, though you'll want to bring your wallet - the convenience of having both hotpot and BBQ comes at a price. Birthday diners eat free with groups of four or more, which takes some sting out of the bill.

Hou Yi Hot Pot

AYCE hotpot

92 Hester St, New York • Chinatown

This all-you-can-eat Chinatown spot delivers solid hotpot fundamentals with an ice cream bar to sweeten the deal. Build your meal from a variety of meats, vegetables, and noodles, then customize your broth and dipping sauces. The drink menu gets creative with options like coconut and lychee that pair nicely with the rich, savory broths.

It's a bit of a hole-in-the-wall situation that can get crowded - expect a 30-minute wait during peak times. The casual vibe works well for relaxed dinner dates or group outings where you want to linger over bubbling pots without any pretension.

Da Long Yi Hot Pot

upscale mala hotpot

159 Canal St 2nd floor, New York • Chinatown

Head upstairs at this Chinatown destination for spicy Szechuan hotpot that brings serious heat. Choose from various meats, vegetables, broths, and an array of dips to create your ideal combination. The lively atmosphere and interactive format make it perfect for group dinners where everyone can cook their own selections at their own pace.

The spice level here is no joke - we love it, but come prepared. Festive holiday decorations add to the celebratory vibe, though prices can creep up depending on what you order. The authentic Szechuan flavors make it worth seeking out when you're craving that numbing málà sensation.

chinese hotpot
